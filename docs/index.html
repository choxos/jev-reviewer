    How it works

    Jev, TypeSafe's System One model, never writes an answer. It reads each file a few pages at a time and points at the lines that answer your question; the app copies them word for word, with their file and page. Each question takes two passes: every stretch of every file is screened for all questions at once, then each promising line is checked on its own, so an answer comes back Reported, Unclear or Not found, and one spread over several table rows keeps all of them.

    You stay the reviewer. On each quote, the green tick makes it the question's answer (the other quotes fold away until you ask for them), the pencil opens your answer to edit, and the squares copy the quote with its place. The quotes stay word for word; your answer is what you edit. The Check button under a question checks it as it stands, for example when the files do not report it. The extraction table shows every study against every question, counts the PRISMA flow once studies are excluded, exports the results, and, in its tabs, shows risk of bias, retractions and open access copies, compares your answers with a second reviewer's, and drafts your methods paragraph. Before screening, Deduplicate search results (in the column) removes the duplicates across your database exports that both the rules and Jev find, and lists those only one of them finds for you to decide. Screen titles and abstracts then puts each record against your eligibility criteria: Jev judges each criterion from the title and abstract, you decide every record, and the included ones become the project's studies.

    Projects, studies, files and answers stay in this browser, on this device: nothing is uploaded or kept on a server. Only the text of the files and your questions go to TypeSafe, through this site's relay, which pays with a shared key up to a daily limit. To check for retractions and open access copies, a study's DOI or PubMed id goes to Crossref, OpenAlex and PubMed, and through the relay to Retraction Watch (XeraRetractionTracker) and PubMed Central. In Chrome, speech recognition sends your voice to Google. Google Analytics counts visits, and sees neither your files, your questions nor the names of your projects and studies.

    Enter asks. Find looks up exact words in the files, at once and without Jev. Say a question, or “next” and “previous” to move between quotes.

    Keys

    When no field is being typed in: j and k move between answers, c checks or unchecks the one in view, e opens or closes its answer editor, n goes to the next answer not yet checked, and / goes to the question box. In the editor, Esc closes it. Once every answer is checked, the next study to check is one press away.

    Projects

    A project holds its studies; a study, its files and answers. They are kept in this browser, on this device, and never uploaded. A questions file loaded in a project runs on any of its studies, and the project's export has every study's answers in one sheet.
